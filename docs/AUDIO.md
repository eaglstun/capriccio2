# The soundscape is synthesized — there are no audio files

Source lines ~29760–29900. Everything you hear is generated by Web Audio at
runtime. Not one sample ships in the bundle, which is a meaningful part of why
the whole game fits in 662KB.

Master gain: `0.32`.

## Wind — pink noise with a 14-second gust cycle

```js
noise(8s) → bandpass(480 Hz, Q 0.6) → windGain(0.05) → master
```

The clever part is the modulation:

```js
lfo = oscillator(0.07 Hz) → gain(130) → bandpass.frequency
```

An LFO at **0.07 Hz — one cycle every ~14 seconds** — sweeps the filter's
centre frequency ±130 Hz around 480. The wind doesn't get louder and quieter;
its _timbre_ moves, the way real wind changes colour as it gusts. Amplitude
tremolo would have sounded like a fan.

`windGain = 0.04 + dusk × 0.035` — **the wind picks up at dusk.**

## The noise is pink, not white

`noiseSource()` builds a looping buffer through three one-pole filters summed
with a little raw white:

```js
r = 0.997·r + 0.029·h      // slowest — low rumble
o = 0.985·o + 0.032·h
a = 0.950·a + 0.048·h      // fastest — upper mids
sample = (r + o + a + h·0.05) × 0.28
```

That's the standard pink-noise filter-bank approximation. Pink noise (equal
energy per octave) is what wind, water, and rain actually sound like; white
noise sounds like a broken television.

## Water — proximity-mixed

```js
noise(4s) → highpass(1400) → lowpass(5200) → waterGain → master

waterGain = max(0, 1 − waterDist/55)² × 0.16
```

Band-limited to 1.4–5.2 kHz, which is the hiss of a fountain with the body
removed. The gain is **squared**, so it falls off sharply — you have to get
close before you hear it, and then it comes up fast. Audible range is 55 units.

## Bells — on the hour, and physically modelled

```js
392 Hz  gain 0.100  decay 2.6 s     // G4
587 Hz  gain 0.050  decay 1.9 s     // D5
988 Hz  gain 0.022  decay 1.1 s     // B5
```

A G-major triad, but the detail that matters is that **higher partials are
quieter and decay faster** — which is how a real struck bell behaves. A naive
implementation gives every partial the same envelope and sounds like an organ.

Each partial is also detuned by a random ±0.2% per strike, so no two rings of
the bell are ever identical.

Fires when `Math.floor(hour)` changes, tracked by `lastBellHour`.

## Birds — daytime only

Every 3.5–11.5 seconds, with an 80% chance, and **only while `dusk < 0.55`**:

2–4 chirps at 2300–3900 Hz, 130 ms apart, each a 90 ms sine with a **−160 Hz
downward slide**. The falling pitch is what makes it read as a bird rather
than a beep.

The birds go quiet at dusk. Nobody will consciously notice this and everybody
will feel it.

## Chisels — you hear the city being built

While anything is under construction, every 0.55–1.05 s:

```js
30 ms noise burst, envelope exp(−t / 0.18)
  → bandpass(2400 + rand×1800 Hz, Q 6) → gain 0.1
```

The centre frequency is re-randomised per strike, so repeated hits vary like
a real tool on real stone. Recall that infill construction is capped at **2
buildings at once** — so the chiselling stays sparse and legible rather than
becoming a rattle.

## Why this matters

Every element is tied to simulation state, not to a timeline: wind to dusk,
water to distance, birds to time of day, chisels to whether the city is
actively growing, bells to the clock. The ambience _is_ a readout of the game
state. You could play with your eyes closed and still know roughly what time
it is, whether you're near water, and whether anything is being built.
