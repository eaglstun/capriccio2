the score, without the city

Everything CAPRICCIO plays is generated in the browser — three Strudel tracks over synthesized voices, plus a soundscape of pink-noise wind, water and bells. Nothing is downloaded. Here it runs with no city attached: the sliders below are the simulation the music reads, so you can move the hour, grow the population, or set the chisels going and hear what the score does about it.

Track 2 arrives at a population of 58; track 3 takes over after 20:30. A change never cuts — it fades down through silence over three seconds and comes back in the new mode. All three are rooted on A.

What you are hearing

Track 1 — calm
A minor, about 67bpm: i–VI–III–VII, held as ninths. It takes half a minute to decide it wants percussion, suspends its thirds for the second pass, and borrows exactly one major chord, late.
Track 2 — techno
A Dorian at 124bpm, four on the floor, and the one track where the pad is not the subject — it is pulled back to glue so the rhythm section can be what you hear. A clap rather than a snare on 2 and 4, open hats filling every off-beat, chord stabs on the ands, and an acid bassline whose resonant filter wanders on perlin noise, so the same eight notes are a different colour every pass. Three stages: pulse, drop, roll. The city has to grow into this one.
Track 3 — night
Half-time at 60bpm — exactly half of track 2 — and voiced a full octave below the other two, with the counter-melody left up at the top so the middle of the register stays empty. Dark hats with a quarter of them missing, and a floor tom where the snare would be. Bar one is always Am9; the rest are chosen fresh each cycle from four candidates. Heavy detune, tape sag.
The soundscape
Separate from the score and always literal about the sim: wind gusting on a fourteen-second cycle, water fading in with the square of the distance, birds that fall silent at dusk, chisels while anything is being built, and a bell at 8, 12 and 18.
The arrangement
Stages are on wall-clock time from the moment you press begin — hats at 0:30, snare and counter-melody at 1:00, ride and fills at 1:30. That is a one-way build: it only ever adds.
The form
So there is a second axis, which starts once the build reaches its third stage at 1:00 and then runs for as long as the track does: verse, verse, chorus, verse, chorus, bridge, chorus, chorus — the bridge three quarters of the way through, where a bridge belongs. The chorus takes the full kit and opens the filter; the verse is held one step below it. The bridge is the subtraction, and it is different in each track: track 1 loses its drums entirely and hangs on suspended chords, track 2 breaks down to a pad and a thin hat line with no low end at all, track 3 loses its floor and keeps its hats. Sections are counted in bars rather than seconds, so a change always lands on a bar line — eight bars each in tracks 1 and 3, sixteen in track 2, which is the unit techno is built in. One pass of the whole form is about four minutes in every track.
