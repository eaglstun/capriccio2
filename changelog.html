CHANGELOG

what changed, and when

This project began as a downloaded build with no source and no source map — a single 662KB file that had to be read backwards before it could be changed at all. What follows is the road from there to the city as it stands.

Written after the fact. Nothing was versioned while it was being built, so these numbers were assigned in retrospect by reading the history back. The dates and the work are real; the boundaries between releases are a tidy story imposed on a hundred-odd commits that never knew they were forming one.
0.9.0 8 August 2026 current

The score alone, the pad, and the horizon

  • /listen.html — the score and the soundscape with no city under them, driven by sliders instead of by the world. Two more tracks to hear, and a song form so a track past ninety seconds has somewhere left to go.
  • Gamepad support. Plug one in and it wakes; leave it unplugged and nothing about the game changes. The left stick orbits, the right stick walks the camera over the city or drives a crosshair when a tool is up, the shoulders change mode. WANDER gets the stick it always wanted.
  • The plain runs to the haze. The perimeter used to rise into low hills to hide the edge of the world. It is flat now and reaches far enough that fog closes it instead — a horizon rather than a wall.
  • First person stopped hitching. Three unrelated causes: a ground query testing every structure in the city three times a frame, six vectors allocated per frame, and an autosave serialising the whole city on the main thread every eight seconds.
0.8.0 30 July 2026

The Chronicle

  • Replay. Every action carries the day and hour it was taken, so the city can be rebuilt to any moment in its own past and watched from there.
  • A scrub UI, and the ability to engrave from the past — a plate taken inside the Chronicle is marked as a reconstruction rather than passed off as a record.
  • The past renders as a lower-generation signal: the post pass knows when you are looking backwards.
  • Three new things to furnish the city with — a brazier, a fallen column, a game board.
0.7.0 29–30 July 2026

It becomes TypeScript

  • Every module renamed .js → .ts, and all 137 defineField shims replaced with real class fields.
  • 151 type errors cleared. The compiler now sees three.js's own types rather than a fog of any.
  • Continuous integration enforces the formatter, the types and the build, so none of the above can quietly rot.
0.6.0 28 July 2026

The legibility pass

Seven complaints, all of the same kind: the game knew something and was not saying it.

  • Carvable surfaces carry setting-out scribes, so you can see what will accept a CARVE before being refused.
  • Stone says where it comes from, and shows its cap.
  • TIMBER becomes SALVAGE — the field itself, not merely the label.
  • Billboard headlines measure and fit, and every sign gets its own typeface.
  • Tullia says the thing about water twice, and only once you have struggled with it.
0.5.0 28 July 2026

More to hear, more to do

  • Two further score tracks, separated from the original bed on four axes at once — tempo, register, texture and voice — because three tracks differing only in harmony sound like one track.
  • Requests become procedural: they are generated from the state of the city rather than read from a list.
  • Ambient life, and timestamps on the things that happen.
0.4.0 28 July 2026

Night, grandeur, and the findable citizen

  • Night. The clock runs through midnight instead of stopping at dusk, with the dark hours passing faster.
  • GRANDEUR counts ornament, in two independently capped halves, so neither can be farmed against the other.
  • Click a request and the camera goes to whoever is asking. Click a folio slot and it returns to exactly the view that plate was taken from.
  • Inside the tracking band the image separates into RGB rather than dithering; citizens get their own outline colour, carried in an alpha channel that was going spare.
0.3.0 28 July 2026

The manual and the map

  • A how to play page, linked from the title screen.
  • The simulation, progression, rendering and audio written down — the save format, the nav graph, the infill growth engine, the terrain generator, the placement tool.
  • A record of the cast, and the rules the writing follows when it puts words in their mouths.
0.2.0 27–28 July 2026

CAPRICCIO 2 — the same city, at the end of humanity

Not a sequel and not a remake. Every rule underneath untouched; only the world those rules run in.

  • The ruins become concrete and rebar rather than marble. A megastructure skyline on every horizon, smog strata, a broken orbital ring, satellites on fixed tracks still transmitting.
  • Evidence of life that stopped: landfill, a tower crane still holding its pallet, an outfall staining the rock with nobody upstream, and billboards still lit and still selling.
  • A slow vaporwave score, synthesised rather than sampled, bound to the simulation. The birds became drones on the same schedule.
  • Districts tint their own fabric; dawn splits from dusk; blue-noise dither replaces the Bayer matrix.
  • The folio made visible — sixteen slots, and the seventeenth plate evicts the oldest.
  • A first-run walkthrough in six beats, taught by doing. It advances when you do the thing, never on a timer, and what you build during it is real and stays.
  • Citizens become a population rather than a uniform.
0.1.0 27 July 2026

The reconstruction

Before anything could be changed, the thing had to be read. It shipped minified, with every identifier destroyed at build time and no source map to undo it.

  • The bundle split into eighteen modules — a partition verified by reassembling it byte for byte.
  • Forty-eight vendored three.js symbols identified from evidence rather than guessed, and replaced with real imports.
  • Subsystems, state, builders and the save format given names that mean something.
  • It builds and runs at 662,197 bytes against the original's 662,267 — a difference of one hundredth of one percent.
