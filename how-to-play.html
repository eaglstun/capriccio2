HOW TO PLAY

you raise the architecture; the citizens decide what it becomes

You never place a house. You build architecture — piers, spans, stairs, vaults — and the spaces you leave behind become places people can live. Roof a terrace and someone moves in. Bring water somewhere and the neighbourhood turns toward it.

Everything else follows from that. Your job is not to construct a town. It is to make somewhere worth living, and then find out what people do with it.

The seven tools

Some take one click. Spans and stairs take two — a start and an end. Pier tops snap, so build from them.

tool what it does
ESTABLISH Piers and columns. Everything starts from a pier — it is the foundation a span can leap from.
SPAN Bridges, aqueducts, galleries. A crossing on arches. Aqueducts carry water along their back.
RISE Stairs. A pier alone is a post; connect it and it becomes a way through.
VAULT Roofed halls. The only way to make a sheltered space, which matters more than it sounds.
CARVE Cut a passage or a gate through a wall or pier. Subtraction, not addition.
FURNISH Statues, fountains, lanterns, trees. Ornament — and lanterns are the ones that do something.
INVITE Paint an intention over an area: dwelling, trade, garden, gathering.
INVITE is the strongest thing you can do. Everywhere else you are influencing where people settle. Here you are telling them.

The four modes

BUILD The normal view, where you make things.
SECTION Slice the city with a cut plane and see it as an architectural section, interiors filled with hatching.
WANDER Drop to street level and walk it. The only way to see the place from inside.
PLATE Frame and engrave a view. Your record of the city.

The five meters

Top right. They read only the places people actually live — a magnificent hall nobody moves into scores nothing at all.

ACCESS Can people reach where they live?
SHELTER Is there a roof over them?
LIGHT Is it dark where they are?
BELONGING Does the place feel like somewhere, with quarters and lamps?
GRANDEUR Is the architecture itself worth something?
SHELTER is the hard one. Left alone, people settle on open terraces — so a city that grows by itself will be beautiful and exposed. If the meter is low, vault over somewhere people already are, rather than building a fine new hall somewhere they are not.

Why has my city stopped growing?

It hasn't finished. It is waiting.

How large the city can get is limited by your clearance — the number top left. Start the game and it will grow to about a dozen and a half buildings, then simply stop, looking complete.

Clearance comes almost entirely from answering the citizens. Every request you complete raises the ceiling substantially — the first one roughly doubles it — and also lets you build further: longer spans, bigger vaults. Reach and growth are the same currency.

So if nothing is happening, look at what somebody is asking you for.

The citizens ask for things

Bottom left. One at a time, in their own words, and never in the form of an instruction — nobody asks for a vault. They tell you that rain spoils the cloth on market day, and you work out that the answer is a roof.

When you solve one, you are not thanked. You are told what people did with it: who raced to the top, who planted a bed the same evening, who sits in the passage at noon. That is the whole reward, and it is the best thing in the game.

There are a finite number of them. When they stop asking, that means something.

The folio

Press P, or use PLATE, to engrave the view in front of you. Plates are collected in your folio.

The folio does not grow forever. Engrave enough and you will see what that means.

Plates also earn a little clearance — so once the citizens have stopped asking, recording the city is the only way to keep building it.

Saving

The city saves itself as you play, in your browser. Come back and it will be where you left it.

Begin anew on the title screen erases it. There is no undo for that.
